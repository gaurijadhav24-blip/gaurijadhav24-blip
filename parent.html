Module 5

Parent/Caregiver Support

Understanding learning difficulties

Support begins with understanding that learners may struggle in specific areas while being strong elsewhere. This should be discussed with empathy and clarity.

How to communicate with a child

Use calm language, focus on effort, and avoid labels that compare the child to others.

Supporting homework

Set routines, give clear instructions, and break tasks into smaller steps. Avoid lengthy sessions when the child is tired.

Creating a positive learning environment

Keep the home environment organized, encouraging, and free from constant criticism. Praise effort and progress.

Communicating with teachers

Share observations and ask how the child is coping in class. Collaboration can improve support.

Understanding assessment

Assessment should be seen as information to guide support, not as a verdict or a label.

Avoiding negative labels

Language matters. Use practical descriptions such as “needs extra support with spelling” rather than harmful labels.

Encouraging strengths

Notice talents in art, speaking, creativity, sports, design, or problem-solving. This builds confidence.

When to seek professional support

If challenges continue despite home and school support, discuss a referral with a qualified professional.
